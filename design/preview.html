§ 01 · system

A small palette, used like ink and paper.

Three colors. One accent — the vermilion of a hanko seal — used sparingly: the brand mark, the unread indicator, the primary action. Everything else is paper, ink, and rule lines. Type is editorial: a literary serif paired with a neutral sans and a precise monospace for addresses and codes. Nothing decorative; everything signals function.

§ 02 · mark

The seal is the brand. One glyph, one gesture.

A vermilion square, slightly off-square, slightly rotated, slightly worn — a hanko stamp. Inside: the kanji 巣 (su) — nest. A small, woven, private place. The same mark scales from 16px favicon to a 120px hero, and reads at every size.

§ 03 · web · inbox

An inbox you can read. One column, named correspondents.

Senders are typeset in italic serif, like a byline. Subjects are sans for clarity. Unread is a single vermilion dot — no bold, no background, nothing shouting. Each message is tagged with the alias that received it; the alias gets a tiny color stripe so you can recognize it without reading.

§ 04 · web · reading

The message is the page. Postmark, then prose.

Open a message and it takes the whole canvas. The subject is set as a title. A small stamp marks the mask the message arrived on — the digital equivalent of a wax seal on the envelope. Actions live at the foot of the page, where you'd sign a letter.

§ 05 · web · compose

Composing is a small, calm card. Like a desk lamp clicking on.

Reply, reply-all, and forward share one composer. The "From" address is the alias — front and center — so you always know which mask you're writing through. The send button is the only red on the page.

No bottom sheets, no shouty buttons. A single column, the same serif italic for senders, the same monospace for addresses, the same red dot for unread. The brand mark sits in the top-left and acts as the alias drawer toggle.

§ 07 · principles

Five rules. Apply across the product.

Choices that compound: if every screen follows these, the experience will feel designed rather than assembled. If a future change conflicts with one of these, revisit the rule first, not the screen.

01

Vermilion is precious.

Use the seal red only for the brand mark, the unread indicator, and the primary action of the current screen. Never as a background, never for warnings (use ink), never twice in the same surface.

02

Senders are people.

Sender names are typeset in italic Newsreader — a byline. Subjects are sans for scanability. Addresses are mono. Each type tier carries a meaning; don't mix them.

03

Aliases are correspondents.

An alias is not a folder; it's the channel a message arrived on. Each alias gets a deterministic muted color stripe so the eye can scan by channel. Never call them "labels."

04

The page is the message.

No persistent right-pane preview. Opening a message takes the whole reading surface. Reading is the primary activity; let it own the canvas.

05

Stamp, don't shout.

Actions feel mechanical, like a stamp. Subtle scale + settle on send. Pull-to-refresh animates a small seal pressing down. Buttons don't bounce; they press.

06

White space is structure.

Negative space ("ma") communicates calm and intent. Resist the urge to fill empty regions with helper text, illustrations, or onboarding nudges. Quiet is the product.
